Each job starts in a fresh VM at the first place in its label order that
can take it. A new Linux label puts your machines first, then the providers
your workspace has connected, in the order you first connected them. A job
of a macOS label runs on your Macs only.

## Default labels

Every workspace starts with these Linux and macOS labels. `ciproxy` is the
default label.

| Label                                       | Platform | Size                             | Slots |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------- | ----- |
| `ciproxy` {jsx(Badge, { text: "Default" })} | Linux    | 2 vCPU · 8 GB · 20 GB free disk  | 1     |
| `ciproxy-medium`                            | Linux    | 4 vCPU · 16 GB · 20 GB free disk | 2     |
| `ciproxy-large`                             | Linux    | 8 vCPU · 32 GB · 60 GB free disk | 4     |
| `ciproxy-macos`                             | macOS    | 2 vCPU · 8 GB · 20 GB free disk  | 1     |
| `ciproxy-macos-medium`                      | macOS    | 4 vCPU · 16 GB · 20 GB free disk | 2     |

Like the disk of a GitHub-hosted runner, the disk of a label is the free
disk that each job gets. Its VM also holds the image: with the current
image, a job of `ciproxy` has a VM disk of about {defaultJobDiskGb} GB.

```yaml title=".github/workflows/e2e.yml"
jobs:
  e2e:
    runs-on: ciproxy-large
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - run: npm run test:e2e
```

A label sets the platform and size of the VM, not its software. Every
label of a platform starts from the same image. To use other software, see
[Run steps in another image][container].

## Slots

Each machine keeps a quarter of its threads and its memory for the host. It
splits the rest into slots of 2 vCPU and 8 GB. A job takes as many slots as
its size needs, so a job of `ciproxy-large` takes 4 slots.

A running job also holds its VM disk. Each machine keeps {imageBudgetGb} GB
for the VM images, and a quarter of the rest for the host. The machine cache
uses the disk that the jobs leave.

The agent reports the slots of a machine when it connects. A Hetzner AX42
with {ax42.threads} threads and {ax42.memoryGb} GB has {machineSlots(ax42)}
slots.

## macOS labels

A job of a macOS label, such as `ciproxy-macos`, runs in a fresh macOS VM
on one of your Macs. The macOS image holds Xcode and the tools of the
GitHub runner image for `macos-latest`.

- A macOS label runs on your Macs only. Its jobs wait for a free Mac.
- A Mac uses the same slots as a Linux machine, but it runs at most
  {macosVmLimit} job VMs at a time, because Apple allows at most
  {macosVmLimit} macOS VMs on one Mac. A Mac Studio with {macStudio.threads}
  cores and {macStudio.memoryGb} GB has {machineSlots(macStudio)} slots, and
  it runs {macosVmLimit} jobs at a time.
- A Linux label never runs on a Mac, and a macOS label never runs on a Linux
  machine.

## Custom labels

Add a label with its own platform and size in **Settings**, under
**Labels**. A label has a name, a platform, vCPU, memory in GB, and free
disk in GB, with at least 1 GB of free disk. Sizes use decimal GB, so 1 GB
is 1,000,000,000 bytes.

Each Linux label has an order of places. You can put a provider before
your machines or leave your machines out. In the label dialog, move or
remove a place. Use **Add a place** to add your machines or a provider
that your workspace has connected before.

The first time you connect a provider, ciproxy adds it to the end of each
Linux label's order. A disconnected or unavailable provider stays in the
order, and jobs skip it while it cannot take them. If the order is empty,
jobs wait in GitHub. The order for macOS is fixed to your machines.

- A label name follows the rules of GitHub for runner labels. ciproxy checks
  the name with GitHub when you save the label.
- A label keeps its name and its platform. To rename a label or change its
  platform, add a new one, move your workflows to it, and delete the old
  one.
- You can delete any label except `ciproxy`. The waiting jobs of a deleted
  label stay queued in GitHub, and its running jobs finish.
- After you change the size of a label, each job that a runner takes gets
  the new size. A job that runs during the change keeps its VM.
- When the order includes your machines, the dialog shows how many jobs
  of the label's size fit on each machine at a time.

### When nothing fits

:::note[Sizes larger than your machines]
If a Linux job fits none of your machines, it can run at a provider in
its order that has a size that fits. When no place in the order fits the
job, the label setting **When nothing fits** applies.
:::

- **Wait** is the default. Jobs stay queued in GitHub until a place in the
  order fits.
- **Run on the largest size** uses the largest size of the first eligible
  provider in the order. A provider is eligible when jobs skip it only
  because of its sizes and its largest size holds the complete image.
  The job can get less vCPU, memory, or free disk than its label requests.
  When no provider meets these conditions, the job waits.

## Where a job runs

A job goes to the next place in its order when the current place cannot
take it. Jobs move between providers without an extra wait.

A Linux job waits up to the overflow wait before it leaves your machines
for the next place. It skips the wait if none of your machines can run it.
In **Settings**, under **Overflow**, set **Wait for a free slot on your
machines** to **At once**, **15 seconds**, **30 seconds**, **1 minute**, or
**2 minutes**. The default is 30 seconds.

Each overflow provider has its own monthly spend limit. Jobs skip a
provider while its limit holds new starts.
[Set a spend limit](/docs/guides/set-a-spend-limit/) explains when it can
take jobs again.
